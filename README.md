# Tiger Snip

A small, native Windows screenshot tool written in C++20. Runs locally without a browser engine, .NET runtime, online account, or application network connection. A per-user installer is provided for shared-drive distribution; the standalone EXE can also run directly.

## Run

Double-click **Run Tiger Snip.cmd** or **dist/Tiger Snip.exe**. Click **New snip**, drag a rectangle, and release to open the image with **Pen already selected** and automatically copy it to the clipboard. Press **Ctrl+C** to copy the screenshot and all annotations, or **Ctrl+S** to save a PNG. Shapes, Arrow, Check, and Line have large visual style dropdowns.

For end-user installation, double-click **dist/Tiger Snip Setup.msi** once, then launch **Tiger Snip** from the Start menu. See the deployment and data-handling details below.

The app icon uses an orange tile with silver scissors and a white capture frame. Its master, image-edit prompts, and Windows icon conversion are documented in [resources/app-icon.md](resources/app-icon.md).

The welcome screen randomly chooses from ten lighthearted, workplace-friendly headlines whenever it opens, including **The snipping tool of your dreams!** Each appears as a single headline, without a secondary caption. Consecutive welcome messages do not repeat.

The editor combines its capture icon and **New snip** action in one button, with undo/redo beside it and Copy/Save on the right. Select/Pen/Highlight/Text/Eraser and the right-aligned Shapes tools have separate outlined groups. In narrow windows, Highlight and Text use their chisel and T icons with tooltips. Color, stroke or font size, and zoom controls occupy their own row; text also shows Bold and Box toggles. Eraser shows **Whole object** instead of stroke-size controls. Hover a control for its shortcut or a quick hint; Copy briefly shows **Copied!** after a successful export. The dotted workspace, violet selection states, and circular handles belong to the editor only and do not appear in exported images.

Each of the three toolbar rows has a small chevron at the far right to collapse or expand it. Collapsed rows keep a slim labeled strip so they are easy to reopen; the View menu also toggles each row. All rows start expanded. Their visibility is remembered on close or exit and restored after restarting.

Mouse wheel zooms around the pointer without Ctrl. The minimum fits the complete image in the window and stays centered; its percentage reflects actual pixel scale (for example, 29% in a small window and 50% in a larger window). The maximum is 800%. **Fit** returns to that minimum; **100%** shows original pixel size. Small snips start at native size rather than being enlarged to fill the window.

With **Select** active, an open hand appears over a zoomed image that extends beyond the workspace. Click and drag to pan; the cursor closes while dragging. Annotation strokes and resize handles retain priority so you can still move and resize annotations. Middle-drag and Space+drag also pan with drawing tools active. Panning stops at the image edges, axes that fit stay centered, and zooming fully out always returns to the centered fitted view. Resizing or entering full screen updates the fit; a zoomed view keeps the detail at its center when possible.

The full-screen button beside Undo/Redo (or **View > Full screen**, **F11**) fills the current monitor and hides the menu and toolbars. A slim bottom strip keeps Fit, 100%, and Exit full screen available. F11 toggles back; Esc also exits after finishing or canceling any active editing action. Returning restores the window's previous size, position, maximized state, and collapsed rows. Full screen does not change the copied/saved image.

- Pen: freehand drawing with 1–100 px brush widths and a customizable color palette. The cursor previews the brush's color and actual size at the current zoom, with a contrasting outline for visibility. Clicking a color while in Select with no annotation selected switches to Pen using that color; with an annotation selected, it recolors the annotation and keeps Select active.
- Colors: the toolbar starts with eight swatches, all editable. Click **+** to choose a color from the spectrum, RGB values, or hex code and add it to the toolbar. Right-click a swatch for **Edit color** or **Delete color**. Keep four, ten, or more colors; larger palettes wrap onto extra rows to keep formatting and zoom accessible. Duplicate colors share one swatch. Adding a color also selects it and can recolor a selected annotation with undo; deleting a swatch leaves existing annotations and the active tool color intact. To save an eyedropper color, click **+** and **Add color**. Palette colors and their order are remembered on close/exit in `TigerSnip.ini`; an empty palette is also remembered. Cancel leaves the palette unchanged.
- Highlight (H): drag a translucent chisel brush over text or other content. It starts yellow at 24 px wide; color and width (4–80 px) are remembered on close/exit separately from the pen. The cursor previews the slanted nib at the current zoom with a smooth dark outline and transparent edges. Each stroke blends once, so joins and retraced segments within that stroke stay evenly translucent; separate strokes can build up color. Highlights support selection, moving, resizing, recoloring, deletion, undo/redo, Copy, and PNG Save.
- Eraser (E): click the eraser icon beside Text, or choose **Edit > Eraser**, then click or drag over an annotation to delete the whole stroke or element. Each pen/highlight stroke is the complete mark drawn from pressing to releasing the mouse; text, shapes, arrows, checks/Xs, and lines are erased as whole objects too. You can remove an older mark by touching it while keeping newer annotations elsewhere. A click at an overlap removes the topmost annotation. Fast drags catch annotations between pointer events, and one drag is one undo step. **Ctrl+Z** restores erased annotations; **Ctrl+Y** redoes the erase. **Esc** during a drag cancels that erase gesture; press E again, Esc while idle, or select another tool to leave the eraser. Its ring cursor indicates the contact area. The original screenshot stays intact.
- Eyedropper: click the pipette beside the color controls (or press I), then click the image to use that pixel's color. It samples the screenshot and annotations, returns to your current tool, and can recolor a selected annotation with undo support. Press Esc or click the pipette again to cancel.
- Hold the size **+ / −** buttons to change brush or font size in one-pixel steps after a short delay. Move off the button to pause, move back to resume, and release to stop. Resizing a selected stroke or text annotation with one hold is one undoable change.
- Text: select Text (T), click the image, and type. The borderless typing field grows and shrinks with the text; plain text sits directly on the screenshot without an opaque input bar, and resize handles appear after typing finishes. Annotations use Bahnschrift (with Segoe UI fallback when unavailable). The Size controls choose an 8–144 px font size, alongside bold and color; toggle Box for a rounded background with a colored border. Light text automatically gets a dark box for readability. Enter adds a new line, Ctrl+Enter finishes, and Esc cancels the current text edit. Clicking elsewhere commits the text and returns to Select; select Text again to add another annotation, or double-click existing text to edit it again. Select text to move it, scale it with corner handles, or change its formatting. Finish typing before using Ctrl+C to copy the annotated screenshot; normal text selection/copy/paste works inside the editing field.
- Shapes: circles and rectangles share one dropdown with large artwork previews. Choose an outline, highlight, or dashed circle, or a square, rounded square, highlight box, or filled box. Drag to draw; hold Shift for a perfect circle or square. The main Shapes button (O) remembers the last circle/rectangle choice; R selects the last rectangle style.
- Arrow: drag from tail to tip. Select it and drag either endpoint to resize or turn it. Choose Classic, Outlined (colored fill with a black border), Curved Gloss, Straight Gloss (tapered arrows with a black border and highlight), or Block Gloss (a wide, straight shaft with a square tail, triangular head, black border, and highlight). Selecting a Curved Gloss arrow shows a single **Flip** button beside it. Flip changes the bend while keeping the endpoints, affects only that arrow, and supports undo/redo.
- Check: click to place a sticker or drag to choose its size. Its dropdown offers boxed, circle-badge, and simple styles for both green checks and red Xs. Switching between checks and Xs chooses green or red; switching styles within either group keeps your chosen color. Color controls can recolor either symbol, and the chosen style and color are remembered.
- Line: drag a solid, dashed, or dotted line. Hold Shift to snap to 45-degree angles. Select it and drag either endpoint to change its length or angle.
- Select: drag annotations to move them. Use corner handles to resize text, circles, rectangles, checks, or strokes. Shapes automatically switch to Select after placement.
- Choose a color while an annotation is selected to recolor it. Brush size changes affect selected pen, highlight, circle, arrow, and line strokes.
- Crop (C): click **Crop** beside the full-screen button (or **Edit > Crop image**), then drag the area to keep and release. The surrounding image dims while selecting; Esc cancels. Crop works on any snip, including instant captures, and adjusts existing annotation positions. Ctrl+Z restores the previous image and annotations; Ctrl+Y reapplies the crop. You can crop repeatedly, zoom, or pan to select a small part of a large capture. Copy and Save use the cropped dimensions, plus any enabled border padding.
- Undo/redo covers drawing, placement, moving, resizing, recoloring, size changes, deletion, and cropping. History stores annotations and crop coordinates and keeps the latest 50 edits. Cropping retains one original screenshot for restoration rather than storing an image copy for every edit.
- Fit/100% changes the view only; screenshot content exports at its captured pixel dimensions.

**Settings > Keyboard shortcuts** has two independent global shortcuts. **Select an area** defaults to **Ctrl+Alt+S** and opens click-and-drag selection. **Instant capture** defaults to **Ctrl+Alt+F** and immediately opens all monitors in the editor, including the mouse pointer. Hover over a menu or button and press the instant shortcut to preserve the scene before focus changes, then use **Crop (C)** to keep the part you need. Both shortcuts work while Tiger Snip is running, including in the tray. Conflicts and duplicate bindings are rejected; clear either field with Backspace to disable it independently. Existing area shortcut settings are preserved. Settings are stored in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Tiger Snip\TigerSnip.ini` under `Hotkey` (area) and `InstantHotkey` (all monitors). **File > Capture all monitors now** and the tray menu also offer instant capture.

**Pause** can be assigned by itself to either shortcut: focus the field, press Pause/Break, then **Save**. The field displays Pause and remembers the binding after restarting. Other keys require Ctrl or Alt; F12 remains reserved.

**Settings > Save location...** opens a folder picker. Choose a folder and click **Use this folder** to remember it immediately. Future Save/Save As dialogs start there, and you can still choose another destination for any individual snip. Ctrl+S on an already saved snip continues to update its existing file. The tray menu also offers Save location. If the folder is removed or becomes unavailable, the save dialog falls back to its normal location.

**Settings > Rendering...** lets you enable **Use software rendering (compatibility mode)** if the window freezes, shows stale content, or takes seconds to redraw during resizing or maximizing. Leave it unchecked for hardware acceleration, the default for most PCs. Software rendering can help with graphics-driver issues but may use more CPU on large displays. **Apply** switches immediately and remembers the choice in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Tiger Snip\TigerSnip.ini`; no restart is needed. This affects Tiger Snip only and does not change copied or saved image quality.

**Settings > Professional Border** is optional and **off by default**. Its submenu has **Enabled**, **Blur**, and **Rounded corners**. Turning Enabled on activates both effects; uncheck either component to use just the other. The shared export renderer adds a soft neutral-gray halo (48% opacity, 16 px blur, 1 px spread, 2 px downward offset) without a hard outline, and optionally clips the corners to an 8 px radius. Blur adds 20 px transparent padding on every side, increasing export dimensions by 40 px in each direction; rounded corners alone keep the original dimensions. With both components unchecked, the output is unchanged. The screenshot is not scaled or tinted. The effects are baked into copied images and saved PNGs, and all three settings are remembered on close or exit. The editor shows the same styled image as Copy and PNG Save; changing these settings immediately updates the preview, including transparent padding. Fit and panning include the complete output bounds.

**Settings > Samtec Logo** is optional and **off by default**. Its submenu offers **Enabled** and six visual style previews: White badge and Soft watermark versions of the S, tiger, and full Samtec wordmark. Choosing a style enables the logo; uncheck Enabled to turn it off while keeping the selected style. White badges use a compact rounded white background, a fine gray edge, and a faint halo, with the original artwork at 94% opacity. Soft watermarks have no card, border, or orange underline: they use the transparent mark in monochrome at 32% opacity, with a very faint opposing halo around just the artwork. The renderer samples the background beneath the watermark and chooses gray on light backgrounds or light gray on dark backgrounds to keep the faint mark visible.

The complete logo treatment scales with the screenshot's shorter side, including its halo. White badges use 9% for the S, 8.5% for the tiger, and 5.5% for the wordmark; soft watermarks use 7.5%, 7%, and 5% respectively. White badge heights are capped at 80 px for S/tiger and 52 px for wordmarks; soft watermarks at 64 px and 44 px. All use a 2.5% inset from the bottom right. The assets are embedded in the executable. The shared export renderer places the logo after annotations and before Professional Border, so the settings work independently for preview, Copy, and PNG Save. Enabled state and selected style are remembered on close or exit. The logo appears in the image preview as soon as it is enabled or its style changes, and disappears when disabled. Preview, Copy, and PNG Save share the same rendering pipeline.

A successful Copy (including Ctrl+C) briefly flashes the screenshot with a subtle violet tint and edge, fading out in about 280 ms. The Copied button and status message also confirm success. The flash is an editor effect and never appears in copied or saved pixels. Busy-clipboard failures do not start it.

**Settings > Auto copy new snips** is enabled by default and also appears in the tray menu. Each completed capture, including instant and all-monitor snips, copies the image with the current export settings and shows a Copied confirmation. Automatic copying has no flash; **Ctrl+C** or Copy always copies the latest annotations and flashes again, even when the image was already copied. Turn Auto copy off to leave the clipboard unchanged on capture; this choice is remembered across restarts. Edits, canceled captures, and reopening Recent do not automatically copy. If the clipboard is busy, the snip remains editable and Ctrl+C retries.

**Recent** beside Copy/Save keeps the last **10 captures, including the current snip**, while Tiger Snip is running. Its thumbnail popup shows newest captures first and highlights the current one. Click a thumbnail to reopen it with its editable annotations, crop, undo/redo history, zoom, and save path; the popup closes so the full canvas remains available. Editing, copying, saving, or revisiting a snip keeps its original place in capture order. Capture 11 removes the oldest entry. Scroll the popup or use Newer/Older in smaller windows. Arrow keys, Home/End, and Page Up/Page Down browse thumbnails; Enter/Space selects one. Esc or clicking outside dismisses the popup without editing the image. **View > Recent snips** and **Ctrl+Shift+R** work with collapsed toolbars; full screen has a Recent button in its bottom bar. Thumbnails include committed edits and are cached; only the active snip retains a full-size rendered preview. The capture collection is kept in memory, never written as an editable project or restored after a restart.

Closing the editor commits active text, retains recent captures, and leaves Tiger Snip in the tray. Click its tray icon to snip; right-click and choose Open editor to access Recent. **File > Exit** quits completely and clears the collection. Closing, exiting, and starting a new snip proceed immediately without a save confirmation. Copy or Save explicitly exports your annotations. Canceling screen selection with Esc restores the previous snip and its annotations without adding an entry. **Settings > Run at sign-in** is optional and off by default; it registers this executable under your own Windows account and starts it quietly in the tray. Keep the executable at the same location when using that setting, and turn the setting off before moving or deleting it.

Each tool remembers its own previous color (including custom colors) and shape style. The shared pen/shape stroke width (1–100 px) is also remembered, independently of highlight width and text size. Text also remembers font size, bold, and Box; Shapes remembers whether you last used a circle or rectangle. Recoloring a selected annotation also updates the remembered color for its tool. These preferences are saved in `TigerSnip.ini` on close or exit only when changed, and restored on the next launch; drawing does not write settings to disk. New captures still start with Pen, and shapes still switch to Select after placement. Stroke width is stored as `StrokeWidth` under `[ToolPreferences]`; existing settings without it start at 4 px.

PNG saves contain the screenshot and annotations in one image, retaining transparency when Professional Border is enabled. They can be viewed in any image app; this version does not save an editable project format. Clipboard output offers PNG first, followed by DIBV5 with alpha metadata and DIB for compatibility with common paste destinations. The destination application chooses which clipboard format it uses.

For shared-drive deployment, distribute **Tiger Snip Setup.msi**. Each user double-clicks it once, then opens **Tiger Snip** from the Start menu. Setup installs a local copy in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\Tiger Snip`, registers it for removal through Windows Installed apps, and requires no administrator elevation. Corporate policies may still restrict per-user installations; IT should test the package on a representative managed PC. It does not enable startup automatically. Use **Settings > Run at sign-in** if wanted.

Normal preferences are saved separately in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Tiger Snip\TigerSnip.ini`, within the current Windows user's profile. Users never write preferences to the shared release folder. Setup contains no personal INI or captures. On the first ordinary launch, an existing adjacent `TigerSnip.ini` is imported only if personal preferences do not exist. The standalone EXE still runs directly without installation and uses the same per-user preferences. Disable **Run at sign-in** before uninstalling if you enabled it. Removing the app keeps personal preferences; delete that settings folder separately for a complete reset. Updating this one-time release requires a new tested package; automatic updates are not included.

Tiger Snip has no application networking or telemetry. Recent captures, original crop pixels, and undo history live in the local process's memory until Exit or eviction of the oldest capture. Closing to the tray retains them. Copy places an image on the Windows clipboard, which can outlive Tiger Snip and is subject to Windows clipboard history/synchronization settings and other applications. Save writes a PNG to the chosen location, which may itself be a network or synchronized folder. An interrupted save can leave an adjacent temporary or recovery file. Each save uses a unique GUID name, so a leftover does not block a later save. Normal failure removes only the current attempt's staging file; older leftovers/recovery files are retained for deliberate inspection and removal. Eraser removes annotations and Highlight is translucent; neither erases the underlying screenshot. Cropping remains reversible in session history. These editing tools should not be treated as permanent redaction controls.

Overwriting a PNG preserves its existing file permissions instead of applying the folder's potentially broader permissions. If Windows cannot preserve permissions or replace a locked/read-only file, Save reports failure. New files inherit the destination folder's permissions. Save uses an adjacent recovery copy during replacement and retains it if an exceptional failure prevents restoring the original.

## Capture behavior

Captures use one frozen image of the Windows virtual desktop, supporting monitors above or to the left of the primary monitor and selection across monitor boundaries. The app is Per-Monitor DPI Aware V2. Selection and export use physical screenshot pixels; UI scaling and zoom are separate. Both global shortcuts freeze the desktop immediately after removing the editor's pixels through compositor cloaking, before hiding the editor or changing focus. This preserves transient hover menus that disappear when capture takes focus. Area selection uses that same frozen frame; instant capture skips selection and includes the visible mouse pointer at its original screen position. An instant capture uses the entire virtual desktop bounding rectangle, including gaps between displays. Toolbar/menu area captures retain their existing preparation delay to avoid editor fade artifacts. Esc during area selection returns to the previous image.

This version captures rectangular areas of the SDR desktop. HDR color fidelity, scrolling screenshots, recording, and capture of protected content are outside its scope. Image processing happens only when needed, with no continuous capture or render loop. Large multi-monitor captures temporarily need additional buffers during selection and export.

## Build

With a portable [LLVM-MinGW toolchain](https://github.com/mstorsjo/llvm-mingw/releases), run:

```powershell
.\build.ps1 -CompilerDirectory 'C:\path\to\llvm-mingw\bin' -Test
```

The script also discovers an extracted `llvm-mingw-*` directory under `.tools`. The result is `dist/Tiger Snip.exe`, with the C++ runtime linked statically. LLVM-MinGW is only a build dependency.

The verified compiler and archive checksum are documented in [BUILD-TOOLCHAIN.md](BUILD-TOOLCHAIN.md). Builds write `dist/Tiger Snip Build.json` with compiler/options/source-input and EXE hashes; packaging checks those hashes to reject changed-source/stale-EXE combinations.

After building and testing, run `./package.ps1` on Windows to create `dist/Tiger Snip Setup.msi`. It uses the Windows `makecab.exe` utility and Windows Installer automation, embeds the EXE, Quick Start, and runtime notices, and excludes personal preferences. `dist/Tiger Snip Release.txt` records package/payload/build-input SHA256 hashes and the Git base/working-tree state. No downloaded installer compiler is required. This package definition is for version 1.0.2; future upgrades need intentional product/version handling. MSI installation and removal should be validated whenever packaging changes.

When replacing a previously installed development build of this unsigned 1.0.2 package, uninstall the previous copy and install the new package. Personal preferences remain. Windows SecureRepair can reject an attempt to use repair/reinstallation flags with a changed unsigned package; do not weaken Windows policy to work around it.

Alternatively, in a Visual Studio developer shell with C++ Build Tools and CMake:

```powershell
cmake -S . -B build-msvc -A x64
cmake --build build-msvc --config Release
ctest --test-dir build-msvc -C Release --output-on-failure
```

Windows SDK libraries: Win32, Direct2D, DirectWrite, WIC, shell, common controls, common dialogs, and DWM. The icon and DPI manifest are embedded in the executable.

## Verification
Developer test modes create a new `test-output/{GUID}` folder for every invocation. `--test-output <folder>` chooses the output root. Reports check open/write/close errors and failures return nonzero; old reports are never reused. Restart verification needs `--test-fixture <producer-run-folder>`; its own report still goes into a new folder. Tests may create real desktop screenshots: review/delete their run folders as appropriate. The build wrapper prints its output path; CTest gives each test a fresh working folder.

`build/robustness_test.exe` adds isolated regression tests for palette limits, malformed registry strings, late graphics initialization failure/retry, callback exceptions, API error codes, atomic preference updates, pending-save retries, damaged/read-only settings, and failed report output. Fault injection is compiled only into this test target. Run console test executables through `scripts/run-test.ps1` to give each invocation a fresh folder.

Source modules: `model` (document/history), `graphics` (drawing/export), `settings` (per-user storage/transactions), `windows_support` (startup/errors/callback boundaries), `color_picker` (color dialog), `capture`, `clipboard`, `file_io`, and `test_reports`. `main.cpp` owns the existing editor, commands, and diagnostic drivers. Moving those drivers to separate test targets is the next organization step.

`build/settings_test.exe` (included in `build.ps1 -Test` and CMake) checks two independent synthetic user profiles, one-time import from read-only portable preferences, preservation of existing personal choices, normal defaults for new users, and no writes back to the release folder. It uses only its own fixtures. Actual settings location is selected by Windows' current-user LocalAppData API; an IT pilot should also validate separate real Windows accounts.

`build/file_save_test.exe` (included in `build.ps1 -Test` and CMake) verifies new/repeated saves, preservation of a protected current-user-only file DACL, locked/read-only failure preservation, temporary cleanup, existing recovery-file protection, and invalid destinations. It also checks forced termination after staging, private crash-leftover permissions, later save success without sweeping leftovers, and scoped exception cleanup. It creates and removes synthetic fixtures in its own working-directory subfolder without touching saved screenshots. The permission tests require a filesystem supporting Windows file ACLs; network-share permissions still require validation on the intended share.

`Tiger Snip.exe --self-test` runs native model and rendering tests without changing the clipboard. It checks undo/redo and cancellation, hit testing, coordinate transforms, crop boundaries, object resizing, all annotation types, all six check/X styles at small and large sizes, solid/dashed/dotted line exports, outlined/curved/straight/block gloss arrow artwork and selection, moved annotations, image color sampling, plain/bold/multiline/boxed text, rectangle styles, native pen cursor size/color/hotspot across brush widths, zoom levels, and DPI scales, and pixel-perfect PNG encode/decode round trips. Professional Border checks cover default OFF, independent blur/rounding combinations, transparent padding only with blur, neutral halo falloff and visible contrast on white/black backgrounds without a hard outline, unchanged screenshot pixels, tiny snips, and transparency through PNG encoding. It writes `self-test-results.txt`, `annotation-style-preview.png`, `text-shape-preview.png`, `check-x-style-preview.png`, `professional-border.png`, and `professional-border-preview.png` and `professional-border-dark-preview.png` (OFF/ON against white/black backgrounds) to its unique run directory and returns a nonzero exit code on failure.

`build/clipboard_test.exe` (compiled with `build.ps1 -Test`, or through CMake) tests actual Windows clipboard output on a private, noninteractive window station. It verifies all three formats, PNG-first ordering, pixel orientation, alpha/color metadata, and PNG round-trip fidelity with Professional Border OFF and ON without changing your clipboard. Run outside a restrictive sandbox if Windows denies access to window stations.

`build/autocopy_test.exe` (also included in `build.ps1 -Test` and CMake) tests default-on auto copy, persisted OFF/ON settings and menu checkmarks, capture/selection completion, actual PNG/DIB exports, Ctrl+C and repeated copying of already-copied snips, copying annotations, canceled captures and Recent/diagnostic isolation, and busy-clipboard recovery. It uses a private window station and `auto-copy-settings.ini` in its working directory; normal preferences and the user's clipboard are untouched. The desktop smoke suite checks the visible Copy flash.

`build/text_edit_test.exe` (included in `build.ps1 -Test` and CMake) benchmarks 30 synchronous characters for plain/boxed text and boxed text with border/logo effects on a 4K fixture. On a private desktop/clipboard it checks complete field-background pixels, cached/full-render equality, shrink/grow, selection, wrapping, formatting, overlapping annotations, cancellation/history, crop/zoom/pan/DPI, styled edge/logo fallbacks, final PNG and clipboard payloads, and editing-resource cleanup.
